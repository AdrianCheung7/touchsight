Stage 2

What counts as synergy

Working definition

Two or three proven winners, each supplying a capability the others lack, that together give one user with one problem an outcome none of them could deliver alone, joined through a single clean interface.

The test sentence: "Only the combination lets <user> do X, because A provides P1, which B needs in order to do P2." If the honest sentence is "A, plus a feature from B", it is not synergy and it is discarded.

0–2 ptsComplementarity

Each parent fills a different slot: sensing, reasoning, or action and trust.

0–3 ptsEmergence

A new user outcome. Scoring zero here caps the total at 5, which fails the gate.

0–2 ptsOne story

One named user and one flow that fits a 3-minute demo.

0–2 ptsClean joint

The parts meet at one schema, event stream or shared object.

0–1 ptDouble validation

Parents come from at least two different events.

Seven fusions failed the gate. Most of them stacked three projects that did the same job (three agent-memory projects, three screen agents). Three more passed but were cut for cost or demo risk: a hardware protocol decoder needed frontier-model reasoning, paper replication needed a paid model, and blind navigation needed a physical walk through the venue.

Stage 3

The decision matrix

Each contender is scored 1–10 on Hackathon fit (matches what judges rewarded, playable live), Idea power (problem size, novelty against existing products, use after the event) and Vibe-coding feasibility (1–3 people, 48 hours, standard web stack, $0 runtime, reliable demo). Move the weights to see how stable the ranking is.

Weights are normalised to 100%. Ties break on feasibility, then idea power, which is the rule the analyst set before scoring. Figures under each name are fit / power / feasibility.

At least seven live lease checkers and five scam-detection apps exist, one of them already marketed as "self-learning".

Stage 4

The top three

Score 7.3 · fit 8 · power 7 · feasibility 7

Turns any textbook diagram into something a blind student can explore with a fingertip in 60 seconds, with no embosser, no sighted helper and no internet.

Dots · LA Hacks 2026ROSS · Hack the NorthGemma Vision · Gemma 3n 1st

Why it wins

  1. Accessibility with a named user is the most consistent winner across all 15 events: the whole Gemma 3n podium, Netra, Dots, ROSS and most "Most Impactful" awards.
  2. The AI runs once per image, before the demo. The live part is finger-to-sound hit-testing, so it cannot hang on stage.
  3. Every build chunk is standard work: a Transformers.js recipe, a canvas editor, Web Audio and SVG export.
  4. Offline and private is a selling point here, because schools have student-data rules.

3-minute demo

0:00–0:25Hand-made tactile graphics take specialists weeks, so blind students fall behind in STEM.
0:25–1:00A teacher photographs a cell diagram. Regions appear; the teacher fixes two in 10 seconds.
1:00–2:00A blindfolded judge finds the mitochondria alone, guided by edge ticks and spoken labels. Time it on screen.
2:00–2:30Airplane mode on, still works. Show the swell-paper export and QR code.
2:30–3:00Architecture slide: models in the browser, $0 per student.
Beats Say-It-Play-It becauseThey tie at 7.3, and TouchSight wins the tie-break on idea power. It removes a real cost (hand-made tactile graphics) where #2 is mainly delight, so it scores higher on rubrics that weight impact.
Biggest riskRegion labels on dense diagrams. Make the teacher's 10-second correction a feature, pre-test 3–4 figures, and get one quote from a teacher of the visually impaired before the event.
Score 7.3 · fit 9 · power 6 · feasibility 7

Describe any movement game in one sentence and play it with your whole body 20 seconds later. A local model writes it once; play makes zero AI calls.

kinemo · PennApps 1st overallChainCraft · ETHDenver 2026OmniBridge · Opus 4.7 finalist

Why it wins

  1. The strongest judge-plays-it moment in the set. kinemo won 1st overall because judges could play it.
  2. "Model once, deterministic forever" won at three events. It gives a concrete engineering claim: 0 inference calls during play.
  3. The most novel of the nine. No text-to-game tool uses body control, and no body-game tool accepts free text.
  4. Ollama's schema-constrained output makes every compiled game valid, and it can be pitched for games, PE, physio or kids.

3-minute demo

0:00–0:20Motion games need a studio. Physios and PE teachers need custom ones every day.
0:20–0:50Type "dodge falling tacos by squatting, clap to shoot the pigeons". The game appears.
0:50–1:30A teammate plays while a counter reads "AI calls during play: 0".
1:30–2:20A judge names a game; it compiles live and they play it.
2:20–3:00Remix one word, share by QR, then show the architecture.
Beats Receipts becauseOnce a game compiles, nothing can fail on stage. Receipts depends on speech recognition in a noisy room. It also scores higher on fit because the judge plays instead of watching.
Biggest riskA valid game that is boring, or a request outside the game language. Give every setting sane defaults, map unknown moves to the nearest supported one on screen, and keep 6 cached showcase games.
Score 7.2 · fit 8 · power 8 · feasibility 6

Listens to a debate and, for every number someone claims, pulls the actual government data and shows the chart. The verdict is computed, not guessed.

No Cap · Cal Hacks 3rd overallOrbital Assets · Gemini 3OverSEA · PennApps 3rd

Why it wins

  1. The highest idea power in the set. Existing live fact-checkers (LiveFC, Factiverse, ClaimBuster) have a model judge retrieved text; Receipts calculates the answer from data.
  2. It combines three patterns judges rewarded: plain math with the model only writing prose, checking against ground truth, and live real-time output.
  3. It fixes the weak spot of a podium winner: No Cap's verdicts were asserted by a model and relied on paid search.
  4. "Out of scope, no receipt" is honest and reads as a trust feature to judges.

3-minute demo

0:00–0:20Fact-checks arrive days late, and AI fact-checkers hallucinate too.
0:20–1:20A mock debate clip plays; claims pop into a feed, each with a chart and verdict.
1:20–1:50Open one receipt: parsed query, SQL, dataset link. "The model wrote this sentence, not the verdict."
1:50–2:30A judge speaks a claim and gets a receipt in about 3 seconds, then an out-of-scope claim.
2:30–3:00Speaker accuracy scoreboard, then the $0 architecture.
Beats ClearPass (#4) becauseThe demo is live and interactive instead of a document upload. Its domain hasn't already won twice, and it has one hard integration instead of five subsystems.
Biggest riskA misparsed claim makes a "truth machine" look wrong. Show the parsed query before the verdict, use a push-to-talk mic, and make the pre-recorded clip the main input.

Stage 5

Claude skills and a $0 stack

Claude Code and its skills are only used while building. The app you ship never calls Claude or any other paid API. Every model is downloaded ahead of time, so the demo runs in airplane mode. One shared core covers all three ideas, so you can pick after the theme is announced.

Before you rely on this

  • All scores are agent judgment based on the research, not measurements. The top three held under default, equal and power-heavy weights; only an extreme feasibility weighting lets Air Changes and LeaseLint catch up.
  • "Biggest" was applied loosely. PennApps (85 projects) and ETHDenver (186) are much smaller than the online events, and were kept for variety and 2026 coverage.
  • Some details are thin. Several Gemma 3n and NASA project pages load only with JavaScript, so their tech stacks are partly unverified.
  • Florence-2 is not yet confirmed on Transformers.js v4. The plan is to test it five days before the event and pin v3 if it breaks.
  • For Receipts, Chrome's live speech recognition is free and keyless but sends audio to Google. The offline path is whisper-base in the browser.
  • Star counts reported for skill repos (for example ~292k for superpowers) came from the research agent and were not double-checked.
